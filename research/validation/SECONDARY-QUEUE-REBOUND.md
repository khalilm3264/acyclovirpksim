## Case 20 — Blohm: dose-timed samples without an HD course

The patient is a 12-year-old girl with CML after allogeneic stem-cell
transplantation. IV prophylaxis began on transplant day −8 at 10 mg/kg three
times daily. It was reduced to 10 mg/kg twice daily on day +18 for renal
impairment and stopped on day +22 after seizures (pp.1049–1050). Creatinine
clearance declined from 80.3 mL/min before transplantation to 47.5 on day +16;
serum creatinine reached 1.7 mg/dL on day +20 (p.1049).

The primary Table 1 on p.1050 reports the following ACV measurements in **mg/L**:

| Day relative to transplant | Dose context | Time after last dose | ACV |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| −7 | 10 mg/kg three times daily | 1 h | 10.8 |
| 0 | No ACV given that day | Not supplied | <0.5 |
| +7 | 10 mg/kg three times daily | 1 h | 15.2 |
| +14 | 10 mg/kg three times daily | 1 h | 12.0 |
| +21 | 10 mg/kg twice daily | 5 h | 4.3 |
| +28 | Discontinued | No hour-level draw time supplied | <0.5 |
| +35 | Discontinued | No hour-level draw time supplied | <0.5 |

The table is headed serum ACV; the accompanying text describes measurement by
HPLC on stored **plasma** samples. Preserve that source matrix ambiguity rather
than assuming a verified serum/plasma equivalence. The 1-hour and 5-hour
post-dose concentrations are not a standardized peak/trough series. Censored
values remain <0.5, not zero. MRI resolution by day +31 is not an assay endpoint.
No acute HD, HD end time, disconnect concentration or delayed post-HD pair is
reported in this clinical course. Rebound is unassessable, not demonstrated
absent. Ongoing doses intervene among the early samples; later low values follow
withdrawal over days.

## Case 42 — Feldman: five measured washout points, reconstructed prior curve

A 17-year-old girl with metastatic ovarian germ-cell tumor, weight 38.6 kg and
BSA 1.3 m², received IV ACV 500 mg/m² (16.8 mg/kg) every eight hours for HSV
mucositis; treatment stopped after six doses (pp.385–386). The five **serum**
measurements were obtained during the next 31 hours (p.386). Prose specifies
**190 µM at 5.6 hours after the last dose and 25 µM at 31 hours**. Figure 1's
measured triangle labels are **189, 131, 94, 43 and 25 µM**; the first differs
from the rounded prose value. Interior sample times are not tabulated and are
not assigned invented clock values in this audit. µM means µmol/L, not µg/mL.

The smooth upper profile includes reconstructed concentrations for the earlier
six-dose course; it is not a directly measured continuous curve. The lower
profile is an author-model comparator. The reported clearance 28 mL/min/1.73 m²,
steady-state volume 10.1 L/m² and half-life 9.3 hours are **author-fitted**
parameters, not independent measured endpoints (p.386). Page 387 separately
prints actual clearance 27 mL/min/1.73 m²; retain that rounding/reporting
difference rather than silently changing the fit value. The measured CrCl of
27 mL/min/1.73 m² was obtained two days after withdrawal (p.387).

A reduced-dose retreatment two weeks later lasted ten days, with no new serum
ACV levels obtained (p.386). There is no HD intervention in the reported index
course, no end-HD anchor and no delayed post-HD pair. The falling drug-free
washout measurements are useful exposure observations, but do not assess HD
rebound. Estimated early peaks of 290/470 µM must not be relabeled as direct
measurements or added to the five-point measured set.

## Case 31 — Rajan: an immediate treatment pair plus a later multi-session draw

The 73-year-old ICU patient had acute renal failure with intermittent HD,
including three sessions weekly during the ACV course. Enteral ACV **400 mg
three times daily** preceded deterioration the next day; treatment continued
until coma on treatment day 7, when ACV was stopped and HD frequency increased
(pp.305–306). This differs from the secondary table's IV route/400 mg/day/two-day
summary. Preserve that published secondary row as an inventory record, but use
the primary regimen for clinical extraction.

Printed p.306/Table 1 gives **serum ACV in µg/mL** (numerically mg/L):

| Sample | Relative timing | ACV |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate pre-HD | 24 h after discontinuation | 6.1 |
| Immediate post-HD | Immediate post-treatment, per table caption | 3.1 |
| Fifth day after discontinuation | Exact hour and relation to latest HD not supplied | 0.69 |

The original printed unit is µg/mL. Loss of the µ glyph in machine-extracted
text is not a publication unit correction. The first treatment's duration,
start/end wall clocks and blood/dialysate flows are unreported. The narrative
states **two HD sessions by day 3 after withdrawal** before initial neurologic
improvement. Consequently 3.1→0.69 spans further HD and cannot be treated as a
single-session drug-free post-HD rebound interval. The immediate 6.1→3.1 pair
shows a 49.2% concentration reduction across treatment; it is not a measured body
mass-removal fraction. The delayed draw does not establish early postdisconnect
kinetics. There is no further ACV administration reported after withdrawal.

## Case 4 — Ikuta: plotted viral load and susceptibility are not patient ACV

The 27-year-old man presented with HSV-1 hepatitis, creatinine 1.4 mg/dL and
acute liver failure. IV ACV began at 10 mg/kg every eight hours, increased to
15 mg/kg every eight hours on hospital day 7, then decreased to 10 mg/kg every
eight hours on day 12 for possible neurotoxicity; mental status improved over
three days (p.1). Treatment continued at discharge on day 42; oral valacyclovir
began on day 73. These are active-dose changes, not a drug-free washout.

Figure 1A on p.2 plots **plasma HSV-1 DNA copies/mL**, not plasma ACV. Its
2.7-day half-life concerns viral DNA. The **0.29 µg/mL ID50** on p.3 is an
in-vitro susceptibility measurement of the cultured virus, not a patient serum
concentration. No serum ACV measurement is **reported** in the supplied article;
this does not prove that no assay was ever performed. Kenzaka's serum-ACV-positive
flag must remain visible as a secondary-source conflict. No acute HD or timed
ACV/CMMG post-HD pair is identified in the supplied clinical course.

## Case 29 — Chevret: earlier CVVH and one residual plasma ACV value

The six-month-old girl (pretransplant weight 5.5 kg) underwent liver
transplantation. Persistent oligoanuria and water retention required **CVVH
during the first three postoperative days (POD)**; CVVH was stopped on POD 3
(p.632). Figure 1 on p.633 depicts the early filtration period, creatinine/urea
course, ACV treatment and neurologic symptoms; it is not an ACV concentration
curve. No CVVH prescription, exact end clock or ACV assay at its termination
is reported.

The case narrative on p.633 gives the following sequence:

| Event | Day-level evidence |
| --- | --- |
| ACV started | POD 3, 250 mg/m² body surface once daily |
| Dose increased | POD 5, 250 mg/m² three times daily, as printed |
| Neurologic deterioration | POD 5; the clock order relative to same-day dose escalation is not specified |
| ACV assay | **Plasma residual 4.5 mg/L on POD 7, prior to withdrawal** |
| Withdrawal | ACV discontinued on POD 7; neurologic recovery over following days |

The last-infusion-to-draw interval and the exact draw-to-withdrawal interval are
omitted. “Prior to withdrawal” does **not** establish an immediate pre-stop draw
or a precisely timed trough. CVVH stopped and ACV began on the same reported
POD 3, without hour-level ordering; do not invent a clock gap or an overlap.
There is no patient ACV sample at CVVH disconnect, later paired sample after
withdrawal, or reported intermittent HD. No restarted filtration course is
specified as treatment for these neurologic symptoms. The discussion's general
statement that hemofiltration may expedite recovery is not documentation that
it was used again in this patient.

The cited **2.3 ± 1.4 mg/L** target residual concentration is a reference range,
not a second patient draw. CMMG is discussed mechanistically on p.634 but is
not measured in this patient. Thus neither a treatment concentration decline
nor an end-treatment-to-delayed rebound pair can be calculated. Preserve the
original 4.5 mg/L plasma observation and day-level chronology; this case adds
zero qualifying rebound pairs. The correct DOI ends in **00534.x**, distinct
from the excluded BK-virus Alexander paper ending in 00539.x.
