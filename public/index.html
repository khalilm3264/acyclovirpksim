Simulation summary

Which level to look at: start with plasma acyclovir. It is the level the model is checked against, and the only one with published reference values: the label’s steady-state peaks and troughs [10]. Plasma CMMG matters in kidney impairment, where high levels have been reported with neurotoxicity [3]. The CSF curves are exploratory.

Time above the entered threshold is calculated from the median plasma acyclovir curve. These descriptive statistics have no established therapeutic or toxic cutoff.

Neither establishes 90% accuracy in clinical patients. Use Model validation to check held-out measurements.

No validated therapeutic or toxic plasma acyclovir concentration has been established. Published steady-state IV peak and trough values are presented in the drug reference on the References & methods tab [10].

CSF acyclovir

No validated CSF acyclovir therapeutic threshold is established. The optional shaded IC₅₀ range represents cell-culture susceptibility, not a CNS efficacy target [11].

Plasma CMMG

During each peritoneal dialysis dwell, the model uses a constant clearance for CMMG. Acyclovir can move in either direction across the peritoneal membrane.

CMMG level associated with reported neurotoxicity: 2.58 mg/L = 10.8 µmol/L [3]. Not a toxic dose, cutoff for therapy, or diagnostic test.

CSF CMMG

No validated CSF CMMG neurotoxicity threshold is established. The CSF CMMG partition (0.025) matches studies with an intact blood–brain barrier. In encephalitis or meningitis with barrier damage, measured CSF CMMG ran several times higher (about 0.1 of plasma, up to about 1 with severe damage); set Blood–brain barrier to Damaged to explore it. That setting is fitted to one study and not independently tested. Not suitable for clinical interpretation [4][15].

      Residual kidney function over time

      A constant CrCl produces a flat graph. Add a time-varying kidney function profile to see changing residual kidney clearance alongside the simulation.

      This graph displays your entered/interpolated CrCl alongside model-derived acyclovir renal clearance. They are different quantities, both in mL/min. Acyclovir and CMMG renal clearance are exactly zero when the entered CrCl is zero. The linear CrCl trajectory is a user-selected exploratory assumption, not a prediction of kidney recovery or decline. [1]

      Dialysis-only acyclovir clearance

      Net dialysis transfer is zero outside active HD, PD dwells, and CRRT. The peritoneal transfer component may be negative during back-diffusion. Colored comparison lines can remain above zero if other scenarios with active dialysis are overlaid. [1][2]

      Total acyclovir clearance (including the patient's kidneys)

      Displayed instantaneous net elimination = residual kidney + nonrenal/metabolic + net dialysis transfer. During PD, net drug back-diffusion can reduce apparent total elimination. It normally remains above zero when dialysis is off because the body still eliminates some acyclovir.

      Dialysis drug removal / peritoneal transfer

      Membrane transfer rate is zero outside dialysis windows; PD mass is counted as removed only after a drain. Back-diffusion can make net transfer negative during a dwell. Urea Kt/V is not substituted for acyclovir clearance [1][2].

      Peritoneal dialysis membrane transport

      Model-predicted net ultrafiltration and dialysate acyclovir, reflecting glucose gradient decay, membrane transport and completed drains. This is a simplified mass-balance teaching model, not a validated three-pore model. [12]

      Bayesian model updating & 90% interval coverage

      How it works: Enter actual assay measurements from the same dosing and dialysis scenario. Rows marked Fit update the relative probabilities of the model's sampled parameter sets using a Gaussian assay-error assumption. Hold-out rows are never used for fitting and test whether the 90% posterior predictive interval contains each observed value. The posterior is an importance-sampling approximation, not a clinically validated population model. Do not enter identifiable patient information.

      Why upper uncertainty bands can be wide

      Concentration distributions are right-skewed: low clearance, a large distribution volume, variable oral absorption, accumulated doses, and CSF/CMMG formation all stretch the upper tail. A 5th–95th percentile band is not ±10% error. If the effective sample size is very small, Bayesian reweighting is unreliable; add observations or use a better-fitting model.

      Drug reference: antiviral activity and interpretation

      Herpes simplex (HSV-1 and HSV-2) and varicella-zoster virus (VZV): Acyclovir is phosphorylated first by viral thymidine kinase and then by cellular enzymes to acyclovir triphosphate, which inhibits viral DNA polymerase and terminates viral DNA synthesis [11]. This simulator predicts acyclovir and CMMG concentrations; it does not simulate viral replication, intracellular acyclovir triphosphate, clinical response, or clearance of infection.

      Laboratory susceptibility: Reported cell-culture IC₅₀ ranges are HSV-1, 0.02–13.5 mg/L; HSV-2, 0.01–9.9 mg/L; and VZV, 0.12–10.8 mg/L [11]. The selected virus determines the optional laboratory susceptibility range. It does not change the modeled distribution or elimination of acyclovir. These ranges do not establish a therapeutic plasma or CSF target.

      CNS infection: IV acyclovir is used in the treatment of HSV encephalitis and severe VZV disease according to indication-specific guidance. Dose, duration and renal adjustment depend on the clinical context; oral valacyclovir predictions should not be interpreted as evidence of equivalence to IV therapy in encephalitis [9] [14].

      Methods

      Full differential equations, units, and parameter provenance: Read the implemented ODE system and inspect the evidence and assumption table. The formulas include oral absorption, CMMG, PD mass transfer, time-varying CrCl, and separate CSF lag updates.

      Acyclovir (whether administered IV or generated from oral valacyclovir) is represented with a two-compartment plasma/peripheral distribution model, separate exploratory CSF effect outputs, and a formed CMMG compartment. Residual renal clearance and time-dependent dialysis clearance act on circulating drug. The model uses representative literature dialysis values as anchors, but most remaining parameters have not been estimated from patient-level data in an externally validated population PK model [1][2].

      Zero residual kidney function: both the acyclovir and CMMG renal elimination terms are multiplied by CrCl and are exactly zero at CrCl 0 mL/min, including every Monte Carlo draw. Nonrenal metabolism and active dialysis clearance remain separate, and may stay positive. The plotted renal-only clearance is separate from total drug clearance. This mathematical property is not a verified estimate of all clearance pathways in an individual patient.

      Medication schedules: The repeating regimen generates no more than the selected total number of administrations, beginning at hour 0 and spaced by the chosen interval. Administrations scheduled beyond the displayed horizon are not simulated. The custom medication timeline supports a sequence of oral valacyclovir and IV acyclovir administrations at specific hours; an IV dose is a timed infusion, while oral valacyclovir enters a first-order absorption depot. The same plasma, peripheral, CSF and CMMG amounts persist across administration-route changes and dialysis modalities. Adding or changing medication routes does not reset drug concentrations. Oral and IV dose milligram strengths must not be treated as interchangeable. This is an exploratory mathematical implementation and should not be used for clinical dosing [8] [9].

      Oral valacyclovir: tablets are represented with a simplified first-order oral depot followed by rapid conversion to acyclovir. The active plasma drug is acyclovir, and CMMG is formed from it. The assumed fraction of acyclovir entering circulation after valacyclovir is 0.545, anchored to the US label's healthy-volunteer 1-g study. Dose strengths are valacyclovir free-base milligrams; conversion uses the free-base molecular-weight ratio 225.21/324.34. Absorption-rate and relative bioavailability distributions are illustrative rather than fitted; the label notes less-than-dose-proportional exposure across 250–1000 mg [8]. For exploratory input, oral valacyclovir accepts up to 6,000 mg per administration; this is not a dose recommendation. For >1,000 mg, the simplified constant-bioavailability model extrapolates beyond its anchor and may overestimate exposure due to nonlinear absorption. These oral predictions must not be used to replace IV treatment for suspected HSV or VZV encephalitis [9][24].

      HD: an illustrative dialytic clearance anchored to the mean 81.8 mL/min in a small 1982 study [1]. PD: the historical CAPD study reported 3.4 mL/min measured total peritoneal clearance [2]. The simulator now uses a separate assumed membrane transfer coefficient, integrates diffusive and small-pore convective acyclovir transfer into a dialysate drug-mass compartment, tracks fluid volume and drains at exchange boundaries. Glucose concentration changes a simplified time-decaying osmotic ultrafiltration term; aquaporin water-only transport does not directly carry acyclovir. The illustrative transport, glucose-decay, reabsorption and reflection parameters are not fitted to drug concentrations [12]. A PD-to-HD toxicity case supplies clinical context but cannot validate those transport terms [23]. Dialysate-associated drug is counted as removed when drained, not automatically at the time of membrane transfer. CMMG PD elimination remains a separate exploratory approximation.

      Cockcroft–Gault CrCl: Estimated CrCl (mL/min) = (140 − age) × actual weight (kg) × sex coefficient / [72 × serum creatinine (mg/dL)]; sex coefficient 1.0 for male or 0.85 for female. Creatinine-based estimates are unreliable with unstable kidney function, dialysis, or atypical muscle mass; measured residual clearance may be preferred [13].

      Time limits: each user-entered interval is at most 24 hours; ongoing PD or CRRT is entered as successive daily intervals. No treatment overlap is allowed. Residual CrCl is expressed in absolute mL/min. When Cockcroft–Gault is selected, the sex coefficient and actual body weight are used for estimation. The concentration bands are prior parameter ranges, or posterior-weighted parameter ranges after optional measurement fitting, and are not established clinical prediction errors.

      CNS: plasma–CSF exchange and CMMG entry are a lagged partition approximation, not a physiologically based permeability model. CNS drug concentrations, viral susceptibility, and neurotoxicity markers should not be used to identify a suitable patient dose [3][4][5][19][24]. Measured studies in different populations found comparatively flat steady-state CSF acyclovir levels and variable CSF levels during CNS infection; these are important research constraints, not a validated patient-specific blood–brain barrier model [17][18].

      Model half-life: A single half-life is not displayed in the modality comparison because dynamic dialysis schedules do not have a single meaningful elimination half-life. The comparison instead reports the predicted plasma concentration at the end of the simulation. The model may internally calculate an off-dialysis terminal eigenvalue for research diagnostics, but this is not a validated clinical half-life. Published observed half-lives are listed in the drug reference above.

      Bayesian 90% bands: The implementation uses weighted importance sampling of 120 default parameter draws. Entered fit measurements update each draw by a Gaussian likelihood (assay CV adjustable in Advanced Settings), and a separate held-out measurement is compared with the 90% posterior predictive range including assumed measurement error. Posterior parameter intervals and predictive intervals are different. Uncalibrated assumed priors and small effective sample sizes limit validity; there is no claim of 90% real-world coverage without external measured data.

      Peritoneal dialysis (PD) exchange table: The optional prescription table lets you describe each exchange separately. Dwell is how long the fluid remains in the abdomen; fill is the volume of dialysis fluid added; dextrose describes the fluid concentration; and net ultrafiltration is the estimated amount of fluid removed (optional). These entries affect a simplified fluid and drug-transfer model, not a validated prediction of clearance for an individual patient.

      Research analyses: The optional model-fitting comparison uses published average peak and trough values for an assumed 70-kg adult with CrCl 95 mL/min. It is not a patient-specific fit or independent validation. Sensitivity analysis changes one parameter at a time by ±20% to show its effect on simulated results. Configuration files can be exported and imported locally in the browser.

Oral valacyclovir results have not been validated for CNS treatment decisions.

      Evidence scorecard

      How closely the default model reproduces published concentrations it was not fitted to. For each comparison scenario, AyFlux runs the published regimen, including any dialysis, and compares its nominal curve with each reported value. Predicted/observed is the geometric mean ratio across points (1.00 is perfect; 0.77 means 23% low on average). Inside model interval counts reference values within assumed 90% parameter-variability bands from 400 draws. These are not calibrated predictive intervals for independent patient measurements. Group means are not individual patients; single-patient reports depend on reconstructed timing; Boelaert values are the authors' fitted peaks and troughs. Weight, timing and kidney function are nominal where studies did not report them. Calibration multipliers from Research tools carry over.

      This selection updates the scorecard, comparison plots, residuals and CSV. Counts describe published values with equal point weight.
