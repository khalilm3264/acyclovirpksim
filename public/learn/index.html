Understanding acyclovir pharmacokinetics

A short guide to interpreting educational simulations of acyclovir exposure when kidney function or dialysis changes.

Educational use only. AyFlux is not a validated clinical decision-support system. Its output must not determine medication dosing, toxicity diagnosis, or dialysis prescriptions.

What does AyFlux simulate?

AyFlux is an interactive educational model for exploring how assumptions about drug administration, kidney clearance, dialysis, and distribution affect predicted acyclovir concentrations over time. It also offers modeled cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and CMMG concentration views. These are model outputs, not patient measurements.

Valacyclovir is a prodrug converted to acyclovir; oral administration involves absorption and conversion assumptions that differ from direct acyclovir administration.

Why kidney function matters

Renal elimination is an important pathway for acyclovir. Reduced kidney function can prolong exposure, and renal replacement treatments can alter elimination during treatment periods. In a simulation, changes to clearance assumptions can shift the predicted concentration–time curve substantially.

The simulator uses inputs and modeling assumptions to explore these relationships. A modeled concentration cannot establish a safe or effective regimen for an individual.

What to look for on the graphs

  • Timing: when a dose or dialysis session occurs relative to changes in modeled concentrations.
  • Distribution: why plasma, CSF, and metabolite curves may differ.
  • Sensitivity: how changing one input affects the pattern, not just the peak value.
  • Uncertainty: why a simulated band is not a guarantee that real patients fall within it.
