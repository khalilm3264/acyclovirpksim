Acyclovir and dialysis

Compare how different dialysis treatments and remaining kidney function affect the simulated concentration curves.

Hemodialysis (HD)

Hemodialysis can remove acyclovir while treatment is underway. In a simulation, the timing and length of each session affect how the concentration curve changes.

Try keeping the dose schedule the same while changing the dialysis window. The amount of drug removed in practice also depends on equipment, the treatment prescription, and patient factors.

Peritoneal dialysis (PD)

During peritoneal dialysis, acyclovir can move across the peritoneal membrane into dialysate, which is later drained. AyFlux counts acyclovir in the drained fluid at each exchange and when a prescribed PD session ends early. If observation ends during a dwell, the summary reports the drug still in the cavity separately.

AyFlux provides PD settings to explore these relationships. Its estimates should not be used to choose a PD prescription. For historical measured data, see research on acyclovir in continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis.

Continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT)

CRRT typically removes acyclovir over longer intervals than intermittent hemodialysis. AyFlux estimates CRRT clearance from effluent flow, the sieving coefficient, and the fraction of prescribed therapy delivered. During a scheduled interruption, the CRRT contribution to clearance falls to zero.

The default sieving coefficient is 0.95. Measured adult values span a wide range: 0.92–0.98 in three patients on CVVHD (Boulieu et al., 1997), but an effluent-to-plasma ratio of 0.84 on CVVHD and 0.60 on CVVHDF with an older cellulose acetate membrane in one patient (Khajehdehi et al., 2000). Treat 0.95 as the upper end of measured values; the right value depends on the membrane and the mode. Clearance estimated from blood samples across the filter and from the effluent can also disagree in the same patient, so a measured comparison must say which was sampled.

No dialysis and residual kidney function

A no-dialysis scenario provides a useful comparison. If the kidneys still clear some acyclovir, that clearance continues to matter alongside any dialysis treatment.

Comparing scenarios with the same dose and different clearance assumptions helps separate the modeled effects of kidney function and dialysis.

The Patient controls also offer an optional time-varying CrCl profile. You can enter hypothetical hour/CrCl pairs to explore renal decline or recovery, then review the resulting CrCl and renal-clearance plots in Clearance & sessions. AyFlux interpolates these inputs; it does not predict the true future course of kidney function.

How to compare treatments

  1. Keep the dose and other patient settings the same.
  2. Change one dialysis setting or treatment mode at a time.
  3. Compare the concentration curves alongside the treatment timeline.
  4. Check the uncertainty bands before drawing conclusions about small differences.

These comparisons explain the model's behavior; they do not establish a patient-specific dose or dialysis schedule.
