Acyclovir pharmacokinetics and dialysis

A conceptual comparison of renal replacement modalities in an educational concentration–time model.

Educational use only. AyFlux is not a validated clinical decision-support system. Its output must not determine medication dosing, toxicity diagnosis, or dialysis prescriptions.

Hemodialysis

Intermittent hemodialysis changes drug clearance during a scheduled session. In AyFlux, comparing a simulation with and without dialysis helps illustrate why the timing and duration of a session matter when interpreting a plasma concentration curve.

Real-world drug removal depends on factors such as equipment, treatment prescription, and patient characteristics. A modeled session is not a clinical clearance measurement.

Peritoneal dialysis

Peritoneal dialysis relies on transfer across the peritoneal membrane and exchanges of dialysate. Unlike a simplified intermittent hemodialysis session, concentration changes may depend on dwell timing, dialysate handling, and prescribed exchange characteristics. AyFlux offers educational peritoneal dialysis controls for exploring these assumptions.

See the primary literature on acyclovir in continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis for historical measured data.

Continuous renal replacement therapy

Continuous treatments introduce a different clearance timeline from intermittent sessions. Modeled clearance should be interpreted in the context of the particular CRRT settings and any remaining native kidney function; modalities are not interchangeable.

No dialysis and residual kidney function

Always consider the baseline modeled scenario. A no-dialysis comparison can help separate the effect of assumed native kidney clearance from the effect of a dialysis prescription. Changes in residual kidney function can materially alter simulated exposure.

Reading a modality comparison

  1. Keep the modeled dose and patient assumptions unchanged across scenarios.
  2. Compare concentration curves against the timing of dialysis sessions.
  3. Review uncertainty and sensitivity rather than treating a single estimate as exact.
  4. Do not infer a patient-specific dose, safety threshold, or treatment schedule from these curves.
