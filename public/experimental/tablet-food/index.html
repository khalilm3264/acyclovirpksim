Fed vs. fasted, same acyclovir tablet

Research only. One tablet formulation and one meal comparison, not advice about taking acyclovir.

What the crossover measured

What changing absorption speed alone can do

Try changing the comparison curve’s absorption rate. In this illustrative model, slower absorption delays and lowers the peak, but total exposure stays fixed because the same amount is absorbed and clearance does not change.

Model assumptions and limits

One compartment with first-order absorption and elimination. Fixed dose 800 mg, bioavailability 15%, volume 30 L, clearance 24 L/h. These are illustrative settings, not fitted or intended as clinical estimates. Only the rate of absorption (kₐ) changes. Total AUC∞ = dose × bioavailability ÷ clearance. Area over a finite window can change.

The two observed conditions differ in both timing and total exposure. A rate-only change under this model cannot explain both; the crossover alone does not establish an absolute bioavailability or a mechanism for the difference.
