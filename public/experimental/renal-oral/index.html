Oral absorption in kidney failure

This follows the adult oral absorption comparison into kidney failure. The same three frozen absorption models are replayed, without refitting, against plasma acyclovir means digitized from two studies.

Exposure is wrong in both studies, in opposite directions. In Hara (dialysis patients, before their dialysis session), all three models put 0–12 h exposure 5–29% too high. In Smith (chronic kidney disease, after the fifth dose), they put it 34–35% too low at the assumed 70 kg, and 21–44% too low across 55–85 kg.

Switching between the absorption models changes matched-window exposure by at most 2.5%. Absorption shape cannot close these gaps, which come from how the model handles elimination and distribution in kidney failure.

Models

The baseline is AyFlux’s current oral model: one absorption stage at 1.8 h⁻¹. The lag model delays the same absorption by 0.590 h. The two-stage model passes the absorbed dose through two successive stages at 1.636 h⁻¹. Both candidates were frozen on adult data before these replays. All curves share 54.5% bioavailability and the disposition parameters from AyFlux revision 8fc1c9d.

How the numbers are scored

Peak and AUC use the study sampling times. AUC is linear-up/log-down over the same window for measured and modelled curves. The plotted group-mean peak is not the same endpoint as a paper’s mean of individual peaks, so it is not compared with those tables. Ranges in the table move each digitized marker by its reading allowance: 3 pixels, or 0.08 mg/L for Hara.

Evidence limits

Hara 2008 studied 18 adults on hemodialysis, six per ALDH2 genotype, after one 1,000 mg valacyclovir dose. Each group uses its Table 1 mean weight and urine-measured creatinine clearance. Means are digitized from Figure 1 at 1–12 h. The zero-hour point is assumed to be zero because the baseline markers overlap: it is used for AUC, not RMSE. Later points (24–48 h) overlap between groups and are not scored, and the dialysis session at 48–52 h is out of scope.

Smith 2010 studied three adults with chronic kidney disease after the fifth 1,500 mg dose every 12 hours. Each curve averages three simulated people with the reported Cockcroft–Gault clearances of 17, 30 and 31 mL/min. Body weight was not reported: 70 kg is an assumption, with 55 and 85 kg shown as sensitivity cases. The predose value is measured and included in both RMSE and AUC. Smith 2010 was already used to set the CSF CMMG coefficient. This is a separate check of plasma acyclovir from the same study, not an independent validation.

Digitized group means cover no individual patient. These comparisons cannot support individual dosing.
