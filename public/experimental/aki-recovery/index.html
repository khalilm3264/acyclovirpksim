Kidney recovery after acute injury

Acyclovir stayed measurable for days after treatment stopped. Would allowing kidney function to recover explain the observed tail?

Research only. A single historical case and two assumptions about clearance. Not a patient-specific prediction or dosing recommendation.

Haefeli and colleagues reported a 77-year-old woman with acute kidney injury after IV acyclovir. This replay starts at her first plotted serum measurement after treatment, not at the first infusion. It does not reconstruct dose timing.

What is measured and what is assumed?

  • Reported: peak serum acyclovir 229.9 µmol/L; creatinine clearance 8.5 mL/min around maximum kidney impairment; baseline serum creatinine 96 µmol/L and weight 59.5 kg. The graph’s renal marker is serum urea, not a creatinine clearance time series.
  • Assumed: impaired clearance at replay start; approximate recovery endpoints chosen for sensitivity, not inferred from observations. The recovery target is an illustrative Cockcroft–Gault calculation from reported pre-treatment inputs, not a measured baseline clearance. Shading shows where each selected recovery ramp is assumed.
  • Common setup: same two-compartment adult AyFlux disposition coefficients, no further drug input or dialytic removal, and equal central and peripheral initial concentrations. Peripheral concentration and continuous renal function were not measured. The first sample initializes both curves; it is not an independent model check. The other samples were not used to fit either run.

Haefeli WE, Schoenenberger RAZ, Weiss P, Ritz RF. Acyclovir-induced neurotoxicity: concentration-side effect relationship in acyclovir overdose. Am J Med. 1993;94:212–215. Figure 1, p. 213. DOI.
