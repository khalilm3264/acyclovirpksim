Pediatric transplant loss and hemodialysis

A five-year-old receiving IV acyclovir lost her transplanted kidney during treatment. Follow the hospital days in order. When did the first measured drug levels become available to explain the neurologic changes?

Days 2–4 · Treatment begins and the graft is removed

On hospital day 2, IV acyclovir began at 460 mg/m² every 12 hours for suspected post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease. After graft removal on day 4, hemodialysis began. Each dose remained 460 mg/m², now given three times weekly after dialysis.

Days 6–8 · Neurologic deterioration

Agitation and confusion began on day 6. The child progressed to coma by day 8. The report gives no serum acyclovir measurement during this deterioration.

Day 11 · Reveal the paired serum measurements

Immediately before infusion, serum acyclovir was 7.02 µmol/L (1.58 mg/L). One hour after the infusion, it was 182.5 µmol/L (41.1 mg/L). Doses were scheduled after dialysis. The authors did not specify how long after dialysis the first sample was drawn.

Days 14–16 · Results inform a treatment decision

The team stopped acyclovir on day 14 in response to the day-11 results. Neurologic improvement began on day 16. The samples document exposure after coma had already developed.

What can AyFlux reconstruct?

The report gives the dose per square meter and the timing of the samples around one infusion. The child’s body surface area, infusion duration, and individual dialysis session times and durations were unreported. These gaps prevent a unique reconstruction of her concentration curve.

Strong and Hébert, Acute acyclovir neurotoxicity in a hemodialyzed child (1997). The case narrative on page 741 distinguishes the day of sampling from the day treatment stopped.
