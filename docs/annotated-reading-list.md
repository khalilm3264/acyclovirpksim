# AyFlux annotated reading list

The numbered entries follow the [project bibliography](https://ayflux.org/references/). Hara and Manders also appear in the repository or manuscript comparisons. The final section collects papers discussed during the literature review. Each entry describes the study, its contribution and a point to consider when interpreting it. Product labels and professional guidance retain their project reference numbers. Some notes rely on abstracts or the project’s documented extraction.

## Project sources

### 1. [Acyclovir kinetics in end-stage renal disease](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7075109/)

How long does acyclovir persist between dialysis sessions?

Two-compartment kinetics and HD clearance.

The predialysis series helps separate persistent exposure in anuria from elimination during HD.

### 2. [Intraperitoneal administration of acyclovir in patients receiving continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2243158/)

The investigators administered acyclovir into the peritoneal cavity.

Measured peritoneal clearance.

Route matters because transfer from a medicated dwell and removal after an IV dose start on opposite sides of the membrane.

### 3. [High serum concentrations of the acyclovir main metabolite 9-carboxymethoxymethylguanine in renal failure patients with acyclovir-related neuropsychiatric side effects: an observational study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12748346/)

The authors examined serum CMMG in patients evaluated for neuropsychiatric symptoms.

Observed CMMG association, not a validated diagnostic threshold.

A group association can motivate metabolite measurement without establishing the cause of an individual episode.

### 4. [An unexpectedly high occurrence of aciclovir-induced neuropsychiatric symptoms in patients treated for herpesvirus CNS infection: a prospective observational study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6857197/)

This prospective study followed patients who were receiving aciclovir for CNS infection.

CSF and serum observations and barrier effects.

Barrier disruption can change the interpretation of a CSF sample even at similar systemic exposure.

### 5. [US acyclovir oral labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=51052f0e-1a07-405a-a962-981ce9439e02)

The oral product label summarizes antiviral susceptibility measurements.

VZV in-vitro IC₅₀ range and important exposure limitations.

Compare the assay conditions before interpreting any simulated time above an inhibitory concentration.

### 6. [Multiple dose pharmacokinetics of intravenous acyclovir in patients on continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3624115/)

Repeated IV dosing during CAPD allowed the authors to examine accumulation over several days.

Fitted three-compartment plasma, peripheral and peritoneal-dialysate kinetics; numerical parameters require careful model-specific matching.

The manuscript compares day-five predictions with fitted troughs. Daily and five-day recovery fractions require matching collection windows.

### 7. [ISPD practice guidelines and resources](https://ispd.org/guidelines/)

These practice resources describe how PD is prescribed.

PD prescription dimensions including modality, dwell/exchange parameters and dialysate solutions.

Exchange frequency alone leaves fill volume and dwell duration unspecified.

### 8. [VALTREX prescribing information, clinical pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=56a62c21-bead-4f3b-a24e-c3fd0b4a732f)

The label summarizes systemic acyclovir exposure after oral valacyclovir.

Valacyclovir is rapidly converted to acyclovir; reported mean absolute acyclovir bioavailability 54.5% ± 9.1% after 1 g oral dose; tablets labeled in valacyclovir free-base equivalents; oral exposure is not strictly dose proportional.

The bioavailability estimate belongs to a particular dose and study population.

### 9. [NIH VZV guidance for adults and adolescents with HIV](https://clinicalinfo.hiv.gov/en/guidelines/hiv-clinical-guidelines-adult-and-adolescent-opportunistic-infections/varicella-zoster)

This guideline describes the clinical settings in which VZV treatment routes are selected.

Illustrates separate indications for oral antivirals in localized zoster versus IV acyclovir in severe/complicated infections.

Its role is to explain why a regimen appears in clinical practice.

### 10. [Acyclovir injection prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b1bb347d-74f4-4976-8450-a375a271718f)

The injection label summarizes repeated-dose exposure under specified infusion regimens.

normal half-life, steady-state peak and trough, renal impairment.

The historical comparison uses reported group peaks and troughs. A typical simulated patient cannot reproduce the underlying distribution.

### 11. [Oral acyclovir prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=51052f0e-1a07-405a-a962-981ce9439e02)

The oral label reports susceptibility findings for several herpesviruses.

HSV-1, HSV-2, and VZV in-vitro IC₅₀ ranges, antiviral mechanism, and absence of a validated susceptibility-to-outcome relationship.

Drug concentration in blood and inhibition in a laboratory assay are different measurements.

### 12. [ISPD recommendations for the evaluation of peritoneal membrane dysfunction in adults: Classification, measurement, interpretation and rationale for intervention](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0896860820982218)

The authors describe the clinical assessment of peritoneal membrane function.

osmosis, small-solute transport, glucose gradient and ultrafiltration. Values used in this website's PD transport submodel are exploratory, not fit to an acyclovir APD dataset.

A water or small-solute transport measurement alone cannot determine acyclovir transfer.

### 13. [National Kidney Foundation: Cockcroft–Gault equation and limitations](https://www.kidney.org/professionals/kdoqi/cockcroft-gault-equation-estimating-creatinine-clearance)

This resource explains estimation of creatinine clearance from patient characteristics.

Historical CrCl estimator; accuracy varies with body composition, age and creatinine kinetics.

Preserve the equation and units when reconstructing the kidney input from a publication.

### 14. [NIH HSV adult/adolescent opportunistic infection guidelines](https://clinicalinfo.hiv.gov/en/guidelines/hiv-clinical-guidelines-adult-and-adolescent-opportunistic-infections/herpes-simplex)

This guideline describes management of HSV infection in adults and adolescents with HIV.

HSV CNS infection management and distinction between IV and oral antiviral regimens.

Its clinical recommendations provide context for routes and schedules.

### 15. [Pharmacokinetics of acyclovir and its metabolites in cerebrospinal fluid and systemic circulation after administration of high-dose valacyclovir in subjects with normal and impaired renal function](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20038622/)

The authors sampled parent drug and metabolites in systemic blood and CSF after valacyclovir.

Measured CSF/plasma AUC ratios; acyclovir ~0.25, CMMG ~0.025 in a small study.

Compare areas under the curves for the same analyte and interval. A single concentration ratio answers a different question.

### 16. [Acyclovir pharmacokinetics in a patient on continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3717158/)

A CAPD case allowed investigators to measure how much acyclovir entered drained fluid.

Reported peritoneal clearance 4.4 mL/min; single-patient evidence.

The 24-hour recovery was reported as an upper bound. Preserve the inequality in a reconstruction.

### 17. [Acyclovir levels in serum and cerebrospinal fluid after oral administration of valacyclovir](https://doi.org/10.1128/AAC.47.8.2438-2441.2003)

The investigators sampled serum and CSF during oral valacyclovir treatment in people with multiple sclerosis.

Measured steady-state oral valacyclovir serum and CSF acyclovir in 10 participants with MS, without documented blood–CSF barrier disruption; provides concentration-time and CSF-to-serum AUC comparisons, not direct validation of AyFlux.

Disease setting and barrier integrity matter when applying the findings to CNS infection.

### 18. [Valacyclovir for herpes simplex encephalitis](https://doi.org/10.1128/AAC.01023-10)

The paper reports serial observations during oral treatment for herpes simplex encephalitis.

Small observational oral-valacyclovir CNS-infection cohort with serial plasma and CSF acyclovir measurements; treatment efficacy and equivalence to IV acyclovir were not established.

Detectable CSF drug confirms exposure in the sampled fluid. Comparative treatment outcomes require a different design.

### 19. [The aciclovir metabolite CMMG is detectable in the CSF of subjects with neuropsychiatric symptoms during aciclovir and valaciclovir treatment](https://doi.org/10.1093/jac/dkl067)

This study examined CMMG in CSF in relation to symptoms.

Retrospective comparison of symptomatic and asymptomatic subjects; CMMG association is not proof of causation or a validated toxicity threshold.

Clinical timing, underlying illness and patient selection affect interpretation.

### 20. [Acyclovir concentrations in serum and cerebrospinal fluid at steady state](https://doi.org/10.1093/jac/24.6.947)

The paper describes serum and CSF exposure after oral parent drug.

Measurements after oral acyclovir in MS; useful for CSF kinetics context but not a direct AyFlux route benchmark (oral acyclovir is not implemented).

Oral acyclovir and its prodrug have different absorption characteristics.

### 21. [Avoiding acyclovir neurotoxicity in patients with chronic renal failure undergoing haemodialysis](https://doi.org/10.1159/000188514)

The investigators examined acyclovir kinetics around HD after oral acyclovir.

Oral acyclovir concentrations and HD removal in seven oliguric patients; the studied oral-aciclovir route is not available in AyFlux. Table 2 includes five paired end-of-HD and one-hour measurements. Four concentrations rose and one fell.

The dialysis observations can inform removal even where the initial oral dose cannot be replayed directly.

### 22. [Neurotoxicity from inordinate valacyclovir dosage in an elderly woman with stage 5 chronic kidney disease](https://doi.org/10.1002/bcp.70138)

This report follows an individual patient from excessive valacyclovir exposure through HD.

Published single case with blood, dialysate and urine measurements; body-burden estimates remain model-dependent. The 2024 preprint was not peer-reviewed; it is retained as publication history; the current HD reconstruction cites the final journal article. Publication does not create a second independent case or validate AyFlux.

The assumed interval from last dose to admission is a source of uncertainty in the replay.

### 23. [Acyclovir neurotoxicity in a peritoneal dialysis patient: report of a case and review of the pharmacokinetics of acyclovir](https://doi.org/10.12659/AJCR.911520)

The case describes a symptomatic patient who moved from PD to HD.

PD-to-HD clinical case, not prospective dose-response validation or a neurotoxicity cutoff.

Aligning the modality switch with observation times makes the case more informative than a single clearance estimate.

### 24. [Valaciclovir therapy for herpes encephalitis: caution advised](https://doi.org/10.1093/jac/dky568)

The authors examine the evidence for oral valaciclovir in herpes encephalitis.

Clinical commentary cautioning against inferring HSV encephalitis treatment efficacy from CSF concentrations or in-vitro inhibitory concentrations; not an independent PK validation cohort.

Read alongside the CSF studies when describing what an exposure simulation can establish.

### 25. [Population Pharmacokinetics of Intravenous and Oral Acyclovir and Oral Valacyclovir in Pediatric Population To Optimize Dosing Regimens](https://doi.org/10.1128/AAC.01426-20)

The paper fits a pediatric population PK model across routes.

Source for the separate pediatric IV research module; not validation of the adult engine or pediatric dialysis.

Reproduce its IV equations within the pediatric age and renal-function definitions used by the authors.

### 26. [Population pharmacokinetics of acyclovir in children and young people with malignancy after administration of intravenous acyclovir or oral valacyclovir](https://doi.org/10.1128/AAC.01138-08)

This pediatric study estimated population parameters in children and young people.

43 patients; final Table 3 supports a separate pediatric IV comparison. Counahan renal estimates and L/h/m² units differ from Abdalla’s historical Schwartz input.

An implementation comparison requires converting both the clearance units and the renal-function convention.

### 27. [Absolute bioavailability and metabolic disposition of valaciclovir, the L-valyl ester of acyclovir, following oral administration to humans](https://doi.org/10.1128/aac.39.12.2759)

Healthy volunteers received valaciclovir in a study of bioavailability and metabolic disposition.

Mean absolute bioavailability 54.2% in 12 healthy volunteers. AyFlux’s 54.5% default remains attributed to the product label, not this result.

The independent estimate provides context for the label-based default.

### 28. [Pharmacokinetics of the acyclovir pro-drug valaciclovir after escalating single- and multiple-dose administration to normal volunteers](https://doi.org/10.1038/clpt.1993.196)

Dose escalation allowed the authors to examine how exposure changes with oral dose.

Human dose-escalation evidence for less-than-dose-proportional oral exposure; does not determine a universal saturation constant.

A sensitivity analysis can explore the departure from dose proportionality without estimating a unique absorption mechanism.

### 29. [Population pharmacokinetics of aciclovir and its major metabolite 9-carboxymethoxymethylguanine and safety profile of valaciclovir in early liver transplant recipients](https://doi.org/10.1093/jac/dkaf070)

The authors jointly modeled acyclovir and CMMG early after liver transplantation.

50 transplant recipients; only one participant had eGFR below 30. Joint model and simulations support renal-dependence research, not dialysis validation or a toxicity threshold.

The paired analytes make this a relevant comparator for formation and elimination, with limited representation of severe impairment.

### 30. [Evaluation of Valaciclovir Dosage Reduction in Continuous Ambulatory Peritoneal Dialysis Patients](https://doi.org/10.1159/000057621)

The investigators measured late plasma acyclovir after a single oral valaciclovir dose in CAPD.

12 participants after one 500 mg dose; only eight had three timed plasma samples. Apparent clearance is approximate; full AUC, bioavailability and dialysis clearance were not established.

A 24-hour single-dose sample can test late exposure. Repeated-dose troughs require a separate comparison.

### 31. [Clinical Pharmacokinetics of Oral Acyclovir in Patients on Continuous Ambulatory Peritoneal Dialysis](https://doi.org/10.1159/000189332)

This CAPD study administered oral acyclovir itself.

Oral acyclovir in CAPD; do not transfer formulation-dependent apparent parameters directly to valacyclovir or IV dosing.

Its late measurements provide context for persistence, while its apparent clearance includes formulation-dependent absorption.

### 32. [Therapeutic monitoring of serum concentrations of acyclovir and its metabolite 9-(carboxymethoxymethyl) guanine in routine clinical practice](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopha.2022.113852)

The investigators monitored concentrations during IV treatment in clinical practice.

27 patients receiving IV acyclovir. Renal strata differed in dose and demographics; pooled troughs are observational comparators, not a controlled dose-response experiment.

Assess sampling time and regimen before attributing a difference between renal strata to kidney function.

### 33. [Therapeutic Drug Monitoring in Neonatal HSV Infection on Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2014-3380)

This neonatal case includes changes in extracorporeal treatment during monitoring.

One neonate with changing CRRT and plasma exchange. Circuit and treatment timing prevent use as a general neonatal dialysis-clearance constant.

Reconstruct the circuit timeline before interpreting changes in concentration.

### 34. [Population pharmacokinetics and dose rationale for aciclovir in term and pre-term neonates with herpes](https://doi.org/10.1002/prp2.1193)

This neonatal analysis revisited aciclovir exposure in term and preterm infants.

Final analysis: 83 samples from 28 infants. Reanalysis of earlier neonatal trial data; not an independent validation cohort for the original model.

Record the original trial identifier to avoid counting the same infants twice.

### 35. [Revisiting Acyclovir Dosing for Adult Viral Encephalitis Using a Full Bayesian LeiCNS PBPK Modeling Approach](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40262-025-01545-w)

This CNS PBPK model examines exposure at different anatomical sites.

CNS PBPK analysis distinguishes CSF from brain extracellular fluid. Reuses published observations; model code is available on reasonable request, not supplied in this PDF.

A comparison against previously published CSF observations should identify the original data source.

### 36. [Effect of Renal Failure on the Pharmacokinetics of Acyclovir](https://doi.org/10.1016/0002-9343%2882%2990090-0)

This study examines elimination in renal failure after IV dosing.

Six anuric participants; likely overlapping with the other 1982 Laskin/Krasny reports. Treat as potentially shared evidence, not three independent cohorts.

Cross-check participant descriptions with the other reports from this group before pooling observations.

### 37. [Influence of Hemodialysis on Acyclovir Pharmacokinetics in Patients with Chronic Renal Failure](https://doi.org/10.1016/0002-9343%2882%2990091-2)

The investigators quantified acyclovir disposition during HD in chronic renal failure.

Historical dialysis experiment. The reported concentration reduction is not the fraction of the initial dose removed; match timing and the amount denominator before comparison.

Extraction across a dialyzer, plasma concentration decline and mass recovered have distinct denominators.

### 38. [Pharmacokinetics of acyclovir in patients undergoing continuous venovenous hemodialysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9421114/)

Adult ICU observations describe acyclovir kinetics during continuous dialysis.

Three adult ICU patients on CVVHD; half-life, ultrafiltrate clearance and sieving coefficient. Abstract-level values; full text not reviewed.

Match the continuous therapy settings before comparing removal with an intermittent session.

### 39. [Assessment of acyclovir intraindividual pharmacokinetic variability during continuous hemofiltration, continuous hemodiafiltration, and continuous hemodialysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10519448/)

The authors followed drug disposition as continuous treatment modalities changed within one patient.

One pediatric patient; Bayesian estimates from routine monitoring. Abstract-level, qualitative.

Within-person variation offers a useful contrast, although simultaneous clinical changes complicate attribution.

### 40. [Pharmacokinetic profile of acyclovir in a child receiving continuous kidney replacement therapy for acute liver failure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9880359/)

The case describes a young child during continuous hemodiafiltration.

Single 21-month-old on CVVHDF; open access.

Interpret the time course against the specific circuit and patient size.

### 41. [Effects of probenecid on the pharmacokinetics and elimination of acyclovir in humans](https://doi.org/10.1128/AAC.21.5.804)

A probenecid study provides a control time course in adults with preserved renal function.

Three adults with normal kidney function; Table 1 serial plasma means (5 mg/kg IV) are the study overlay and scorecard data. Not used to fit any parameter.

AyFlux replays the control regimen. Distinguish the concentration measurements from the measured renal clearance used to interpret error.

### 42. [The importance of therapeutic drug monitoring in dosage optimization of acyclovir](https://www.klinickafarmakologie.cz/incpdfs/far-202204-0002_10_001.pdf)

This case reports monitoring of both parent drug and CMMG during IV therapy.

Figure 1 is the source of the paired single-patient acyclovir and CMMG observations; distinct from the 27-patient cohort in [32].

Keep the analytes separate and flag uncertainty in the previous oral exposure and infusion times.

### 43. [Removal of acyclovir during continuous veno-venous hemodialysis and hemodiafiltration with high-efficiency membranes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11076113/)

The investigators sampled the circuit at several locations during continuous therapy.

PMID 11076113 (no DOI). One anuric adult (92–94 kg) on CVVHD and CVVHDF; measured prefilter, postfilter and effluent concentrations with derived clearances. Table 1 labels flows in mL/min, but the text and arithmetic show mL/h.

Prefilter and postfilter concentrations address extraction, while effluent provides a separate removal measurement.

### 44. [Neurotoxicity from acyclovir and valacyclovir in patients undergoing peritoneal dialysis: still rare, but still present](https://doi.org/10.1177/08968608261415988)

This report describes neurotoxicity presentations in patients using automated PD.

Three automated (cycler) PD patients with doses and cycler prescriptions; no drug concentrations were measured.

The prescriptions help reconstruct scenarios. Symptom timing alone cannot score concentration predictions.

### 45. [Death delusion](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.39408.393137.BE)

These cases include measurements of parent drug and metabolite around HD.

Two case reports. Case 1 (chronic haemodialysis, valaciclovir 1 g daily) gives paired serum acyclovir and CMMG before and after two sessions; used as the CMMG between-session benchmark, not to fit any parameter.

Keep the patient and session identifiers attached to each sample when calculating changes.

### 46. [Aciclovir-induced neuropsychiatric symptoms – a clinical pharmacology study](https://core.ac.uk/download/70338802.pdf)

This thesis brings together clinical pharmacology investigations of aciclovir-associated symptoms.

ISBN 978-91-7409-994-2. Open access. Paper V (preliminary, single dose) gives the CMMG decline over one haemodialysis session and a CMMG renal clearance fit; Paper I gives serum CMMG in patients with symptoms. Evidence for the CMMG checks; no parameter is fitted from it.

Trace thesis results to the original investigation before treating them as additional evidence.

### 47. [Effective clearance of aciclovir using hemodiafiltration in a patient with neurotoxicity](https://doi.org/10.1111/aor.12774)

The case reports aciclovir concentrations spanning a hemodiafiltration session.

Plasma aciclovir fell 83.3% across one HDF session with an FX800 dialyzer in one patient. This is a change in plasma concentration over the session.

The elapsed time and treatment modality define what can be inferred about removal.

### 48. [Prospective, controlled study of acyclovir pharmacokinetics in obese patients](https://doi.org/10.1128/AAC.02010-15)

The investigators compared IV acyclovir exposure in adults with obesity and normal-weight controls.

Seven patients with obesity given IV acyclovir by ideal body weight had lower mean exposure than seven normal-weight controls dosed by total body weight. The authors estimated exposure for adjusted body weight.

A weight-scaling hypothesis should be checked against both exposure and peak concentration under the doses actually given.

### 49. [Pharmacokinetics of acyclovir after intravenous and oral administration](https://doi.org/10.1093/jac/12.suppl_B.29)

This review summarizes disposition after oral and intravenous acyclovir.

This review reports urinary recovery of CMMG equivalent to 8–14% of the IV dose in patients with normal renal function. An ESRD-specific formation fraction remains uncertain.

Trace a reported metabolite fraction to its population and collection interval before applying it in renal failure.

### 50. [Pharmacokinetics of acyclovir in a morbidly obese patient with renal impairment](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijantimicag.2016.01.006)

The case combines marked obesity with renal impairment.

Single-patient pharmacokinetic estimates illustrate uncertainty in weight and renal-function scaling when obesity and renal impairment coexist.

One concentration profile cannot isolate the separate contributions of body size and kidney function.

### 51. [Population pharmacokinetics of intravenous acyclovir in preterm and term infants](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.inf.0000435509.75114.3d)

This neonatal population analysis related IV acyclovir disposition to maturation.

Source of the postmenstrual-age clearance relationship reproduced in the experimental neonatal model.

Use postmenstrual age in the convention specified by the model and distinguish the original cohort from later reanalyses.

### 52. [Neurotoxicity of acyclovir in patients with end-stage renal failure treated with continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0272-6386%2812%2970234-2)

The authors describe acyclovir toxicity in patients with end-stage renal disease.

Two clinical cases with poor CAPD removal and serial blood concentrations around HD in one patient. Concentration changes depend on sampling times and cannot directly establish the fraction of the administered dose removed.

Session timing determines whether a reported concentration captures on-dialysis removal or subsequent redistribution.

### 53. [Acute acyclovir neurotoxicity in a hemodialyzed child](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004670050379)

This report follows a child receiving acyclovir in the setting of HD.

Single pediatric case with serum sampling around an IV infusion after HD. These pre-dose and post-dose samples describe dose exposure. They cannot be interpreted as a dialysis extraction pair or an adult clearance benchmark.

A sample drawn after a new dose cannot measure rebound attributable solely to redistribution.

### 54. [Aciclovir-induced neurotoxicity: Utility of CSF and serum CMMG levels in diagnosis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcv.2014.09.001)

The report considers the diagnostic contribution of CSF and serum CMMG.

CSF was sampled on day 5 and the nearest available serum on day 8, with intervening therapy and renal-function changes. The measurements cannot establish a simultaneous CSF-to-serum partition ratio.

Samples obtained on different days cannot establish an individual contemporaneous partition ratio.

### 55. [Acyclovir-induced neuropsychosis successfully recovered after immediate hemodialysis in an end-stage renal disease patient](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-4632.2007.03269.x)

The authors followed parent and metabolite concentrations through repeated HD.

Serum acyclovir was 1.6 mg/L and CMMG 5 mg/L before emergency HD. After three daily sessions, acyclovir was below 0.5 mg/L and CMMG undetectable. This is a clinical multi-session trajectory with limited sampling.

Retain below-limit results as inequalities when comparing simulated terminal concentrations.

### 56. [Timely diagnosis of acyclovir-induced neurotoxicity during acute care surgery resuscitation: A rare complication of common herpes zoster treatment](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.92365)

This case includes paired whole-blood measurements around hemodiafiltration.

Whole-blood acyclovir fell from 6.45 to 0.85 mg/L and CMMG from 10.5 to 0.70 mg/L between samples bracketing a four-hour HDF session. Samples were 5 h 40 min apart. Blood matrix, trauma and resuscitation limit direct comparison with serum/plasma PK or dialyzer clearance.

Whole-blood and plasma concentrations should remain distinct in a comparison table.

### 57. [Acute kidney injury and acyclovir-associated encephalopathy after administration of valacyclovir in an elderly person with normal renal function: A case report and literature review](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000026147)

This report follows acute kidney injury and encephalopathy over several hospital days.

Plasma acyclovir was 34.6 mg/L at presentation and <0.5 mg/L on hospital day 10. Hemodialysis took place on days 1–3. The long interval includes renal recovery and does not quantify removal or rebound after an individual session.

Several treatments and changing kidney function intervene between the two measurements.

### 58. [Encephalopathy induced by preventive administration of acyclovir in a man with symptomatic multiple myeloma and renal dysfunction](https://doi.org/10.2147/IJGM.S291320)

This prophylaxis case describes a clinical course in the setting of renal replacement therapy.

The patient received oral valacyclovir for prophylaxis. The authors report the clinical course without measured acyclovir or CMMG concentrations.

The report can inform the treatment timeline even where concentrations are unavailable.

### 59. [Sticking to the script: A curious case of confusion in an ESRD patient](https://doi.org/10.1111/hdi.12534)

This case includes blood measurements on successive days surrounding an HD treatment.

Day-1 pre-HD acyclovir was 8.9 µg/mL and day-2 concentration after one four-hour HD session was 3.0 µg/mL. Exact post-HD sample delay was unreported and no final level was obtained after the second session. These observations cannot assess immediate post-HD rebound.

Uncertainty about the second sampling time limits claims about immediate postdialysis behavior.

### 60. [Acyclovir neurotoxicity following oral therapy: Prevention and treatment in patients on haemodialysis](https://doi.org/10.1159/000187841)

Two patients on home HD developed symptoms after oral therapy.

Two separate home-HD patients had admission serum acyclovir of 10 and 35 µmol/L, respectively 24 and 12 hours after their last oral doses. Rescue HD lasted five and six hours. Neither case provides a post-HD concentration or rebound pair.

Admission concentrations have documented intervals from the last dose, useful for interpreting late exposure.

### Hara. [Influence of ALDH2 Genetic Polymorphisms on Aciclovir Pharmacokinetics following Oral Administration of Valaciclovir in Japanese End-stage Renal Disease Patients](https://doi.org/10.2133/dmpk.23.306)

The investigators studied aciclovir disposition after oral valaciclovir in Japanese patients with end-stage renal disease.

The AyFlux repository documents a separate Hara benchmark reconstruction under research/validation/HARA-HD.md.

The genetic comparison and dialysis schedule are distinct features of this cohort. Preserve both when interpreting between-patient variability.

### Manders. [Continuous Intravenous Acyclovir as Outpatient Parenteral Antimicrobial Therapy for HSV and VZV Neurological Infections](https://doi.org/10.1002/rmv.70206)

This outpatient case series examines continuous infusion during therapy for neurological infections.

The manuscript uses the series in a follow-up sensitivity comparison. The apparent benefit of adjusting renal clearance depended on the error metric.

Continuous infusion offers a way to examine sustained exposure without the same peaks and troughs as intermittent dosing.

## Additional literature review

### R1. [Determining the Effects of Chronic Kidney Disease on Organic Anion Transporter1/3 Activity Through Physiologically Based Pharmacokinetic Modeling](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40262-022-01121-6)

Compared renal handling of four transporter substrates across stages of CKD.

Use the acyclovir model as a mechanistic comparator for residual renal clearance. Audit overlap with the probenecid and renal-failure studies used by AyFlux.

Trace any shared development cohorts before scoring agreement.

### R2. [Effect of Chronic Kidney Disease on the Renal Secretion via Organic Anion Transporters 1/3: Implications for Physiologically‐Based Pharmacokinetic Modeling and Dose Adjustment](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpt.2642)

Collated data for 33 OAT1/3 substrates.

Compare filtration and secretion as distinct processes in CKD. Cross-drug trends support a sensitivity analysis rather than a patient-specific coefficient.

Use kidney-function strata to identify where proportional scaling is plausible.

### R4. [LeiCNS-PK3.2: A physiologically based pharmacokinetic model framework for predicting intracellular concentrations of acyclovir and ganciclovir active metabolites](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejps.2026.107496)

Used rat and human extracellular observations and in vitro conversion kinetics to predict intracellular active triphosphates.

Cite to distinguish CSF parent concentration, systemic CMMG and modeled intracellular antiviral exposure.

An intracellular antiviral prediction adds a separate layer of uncertainty.

### R5. [Maximum likelihood estimation of renal transporter ontogeny profiles for pediatric PBPK modeling](https://doi.org/10.1002/psp4.13102)

Compared pediatric models with and without transporter maturation, including acyclovir.

Use its model comparisons and fold-error/coverage metrics in the pediatric work. Adult CKD requires separate evaluation.

Evaluate pediatric observations separately from adult dialysis scenarios.

### R6. [An Integrated Dialysis Pharmacometric (IDP) Model to Evaluate the Pharmacokinetics in Patients Undergoing Renal Replacement Therapy](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11095-020-02832-w)

Integrated circuit and dialysate information and examined adsorption and degradation in RRT simulations and antibiotic data.

Borrow the separation of observable quantities. Drug-specific loss mechanisms require evidence.

Keep measured circuit losses separate from inferred losses.

### R7. [Population pharmacokinetics of meropenem in patients undergoing automated peritoneal dialysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/08968608231167237)

Modeled meropenem in six APD patients after IV or intraperitoneal administration.

Evaluate plasma and dialysate separately across exchange events. The small crossover cohort supplies a design example only.

Mark the fill and drain times directly on the concentration figure.

### R8. [External evaluation of intravenous vancomycin population pharmacokinetic models in adults receiving high‐flux intermittent haemodialysis](https://doi.org/10.1111/bcp.16334)

Compared four published vancomycin models in high-flux intermittent hemodialysis.
